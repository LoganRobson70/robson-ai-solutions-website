Building Analyst turns evidence into review-ready intelligence.

Assessment capture and report-ready evidence for building surveyors. Building Analyst is an early professional product direction for keeping observations, photos, notes and review context together before anything is issued.

Product direction

Assessment capture and report-ready evidence in one professional workflow.

The product direction is deliberately practical: collect the building evidence, keep it attached to the issue record, and prepare clearer material for professional review. It supports judgement; it does not replace it.

01

Capture the issue while context is fresh.

Record location, condition, urgency, access constraints, photos, PDFs and notes before the site visit goes cold.

Assessment record
02

Keep evidence and reasoning together.

Each item stays connected to the observation, so review questions do not drift away from the evidence.

Evidence trail
03

Prepare drafts for professional review.

AI assistance can help structure summaries and draft wording, with verification before any client-facing use.

Issue model

What stays attached.

The useful parts of the site visit stay together: observation, location, evidence, risk and next action. The goal is a clearer review surface for the surveyor, manager or client-facing output.

1Observation

The finding is captured while the facts are fresh.

2Location

The building area and access context remain clear.

3Evidence pack

Photos, notes, readings and chronology stay bound to the issue.

4Decision context

Risk, urgency and possible next steps can be reviewed with the evidence close by.

Fit and boundaries

Best where evidence and analysis need structure.

Building Analyst is strongest where assessment evidence, analysis and reporting material are scattered. It keeps the surveyor responsible while making the supporting record easier to review.

Strong fit

Surveying, estates, FM, housing, compliance and public-sector operations.

Why it matters

Useful when evidence, analysis and reporting are separated across notes, folders and memory.

Boundary

Not autonomous diagnosis and not a substitute for professional judgement.

What good looks like

The surveyor can review building information with evidence and context close together.
